The patient is a 45-year-old male with a BMI of 32, presenting with persistent fatigue, increased thirst, and frequent urination for two weeks, along with a fasting blood glucose level of 180 mg/dL, which is above the normal range and suggests hyperglycemia. The symptoms of polyuria, polydipsia, and fatigue are consistent with diabetes mellitus, and the elevated fasting glucose supports this. Given his age, BMI, and the absence of typical features of type 1 diabetes such as a history of insulin dependence or ketoacidosis, type 2 diabetes is more likely. Metformin is the first-line pharmacological treatment for type 2 diabetes, as it improves insulin sensitivity and reduces hepatic glucose production. However, it is important to rule out other conditions such as diabetes insipidus, which presents with similar symptoms but is associated with normal or low glucose levels and responds to desmopressin. Cushing's syndrome, while associated with metabolic changes, typically presents with central obesity, hypertension, and hirsutism, and is not primarily characterized by polyuria and polydipsia. To confirm the diagnosis, further testing such as an oral glucose tolerance test, HbA1c, and assessment of C-peptide levels may be necessary. If the diagnosis is type 2 diabetes, initiating metformin is appropriate, but if the patient has contraindications to metformin or if the diagnosis is uncertain, additional diagnostic workup or alternative treatments may be required.
