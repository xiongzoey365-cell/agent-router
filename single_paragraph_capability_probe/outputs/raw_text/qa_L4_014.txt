The 2018 US Supreme Court case South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc. overturned the physical presence rule established in the 1992 precedent Quill Corp. v. North Dakota. The key constitutional principle reinterpreted in this case was the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, specifically regarding the ability of states to impose sales tax obligations on out-of-state sellers. The Court's decision, which involved a shift from the traditional physical presence standard to a more modern "economic presence" standard, had a significant impact on small e-commerce businesses selling across state lines by allowing states to collect sales taxes from these businesses even if they did not have a physical presence in the state, thereby increasing the tax obligations for such businesses and potentially affecting their operational costs and market reach. To further understand the implications of this decision, it would be useful to examine the specific economic presence criteria established by the Court, the extent to which small businesses were affected by the new tax collection requirements, and the subsequent legislative and regulatory responses aimed at simplifying compliance for these businesses.
